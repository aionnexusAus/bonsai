Projects

What you are building

Not “some bonsai someday” — three named outcomes. Loquat is out of scope. Never mix species in one pot.

Project 1

Elm forest

Goal
One Chinese elm forest (yose-ue) that reads as a small grove from 2 metres away, finished height about 60–90 cm.
Material
Only matching Chinese elms (Ulmus parvifolia) — small serrated oval leaves, thin wiry stems. Use 5 or 7 of the best pots. Extra elms = backups / future solos.
Done when
Trees share one container; heights are tiered (hero / mid / low); trunks have visible girth; you can tip-prune pads instead of growing raw whips.
Oct now
Sort & label elms, harden outdoors, keep in nursery pots or step into a shared grow box without heavy root cutting, light tip prune, dilute feed if healthy.
Defer
Full root prune, final forest spacing, heavy trunk chops → Aug–mid Sep 2027.

Project 2

Oak solos

Goal
Each English oak as its own informal-upright tree, finished about 60–90 cm with a thicker trunk than today’s pencil whip.
Material
English oak (Quercus robur) — deeply lobed leaves. One project per oak pot. Never plant into the elm forest.
Done when
Trunk shows taper; a clear front; lower branches planned; leaf size still large but tree proportions read as a miniature oak, not a stick.
Oct now
Label each oak, harden outdoors, optional upsize into a larger grow pot with only light teasing of outer roots (no hard root prune), let a top leader run to thicken the trunk.
Defer
Structural trunk chop and serious root work → Aug–mid Sep 2027.

Project 3

Feijoa solo

Goal
One feijoa (pineapple guava) as a solo evergreen bonsai, finished about 60–90 cm — informal upright or multi-trunk shrub-tree look.
Material
Feijoa (Acca sellowiana) — oval leaves, green on top, silvery felt underneath. Melbourne-hardy evergreen. Solo only — never into the elm forest.
Done when
Clear trunk line and front; denser branching after tip work; still may flower/fruit at this size (bonus, not required).
Oct now
Label P3-Feijoa, harden outdoors, full sun once toughened, dilute feed (spring is its growth window), light tip prune only. Optional gentle slip-pot if rootbound.
Defer
Hard root prune → early spring Sep 2027 (evergreen window, not mid-winter). Bigger structure prune after flowering/fruit if you want blooms first, or Sep with root work if shaping first.

ID cheat sheet

How to split the pile

  • Elm → Project 1: tiny oval leaves, fine teeth, wiry stems.
  • Oak → Project 2: big lobed leaves, straighter whip trunks.
  • Feijoa → Project 3: oval leaves with silver/white undersides.
  • Loquat → not a project: large glossy serrated leaves — set aside.

Soil · budget

Drain fast, hold a little

Nursery bark mix stays wet too long in shallow pots. Melbourne summer then cooks roots that stay soggy overnight.

PartsMaterialRole
2Pine bark fines / orchid barkOrganic hold
2Scoria or blue metal 5–10 mmAir + heat buffer
1Coarse sand / crushed quartzDrainage

Optional +½ zeolite or perlite. Skip bagged garden soil. Upgrade later to akadama/pumice/lava if you want.

Pots · budget

Grow boxes before ceramics

Your trunks are pencil-thin. A tiny bonsai pot now freezes that forever. Build girth for 2–4 years first.

  • Now–year 2: colander, laundry basket, or timber crate (~40×30×15 cm) with mesh over holes — $0–25.
  • Forest grow-out: one wide shallow mushroom box / crate for 5–7 elms — $0–15.
  • Training pot: plastic bonsai pot or drilled terracotta — $5–20.
  • Display later: op-shop ceramic — $10–40.

Elevate on bricks/timber. No standing water in saucers. Concrete radiates summer heat into roots.

Pruning · stems

Chop for taper, then densify

Most of these are tall whips already near your finished height — but without trunk thickness. Height without taper reads as a stick, not a tree.

  • P1 Elm (Aug 2027): staggered chops — low / mid / hero. Leave a node facing the next leader.
  • P2 Oak (Aug 2027): one structural chop; until then let a sacrifice leader run to thicken the trunk.
  • P3 Feijoa (Sep 2027 / after fruit): shorten long legs; keep silver undersides visible in the silhouette — tip prune for density.
  • Oct–summer maintenance (all): tip prune long soft shoots only.

Pruning · roots

Build a flat feeder mat

Goal is a shallow, radial root plate that fits a future bonsai pot — not a deep nursery tap.

StepActionMelbourne tip
1. Timing Repot young trees every 1–2 years P1/P2: Aug–early Sep. P3 feijoa: Sep (evergreen) — avoid mid-winter hard root work.
2. Unpot Tease circling roots; wash peat gradually over 1–2 seasons if weak Don’t strip every last speck of soil on a stressed whip.
3. Cut Remove thick downward / circling roots; keep fine feeders First session: cut about 30–40% of root mass max.
4. Replant Fresh free-draining mix; water thoroughly Shade 1–2 weeks; no fertiliser until new growth shows.

Training

Wire lightly, grow girth hard

  • Year 1: sun harden, survive, one structural chop each. Optional soft aluminium wire on 1–2 trunks; check weekly in spring.
  • Years 2–3: let sacrifice branches run to thicken trunks, then chop. Start secondary branching. Slide forest trees while still in the grow box.
  • Years 3–5: move strongest material into shallower training pots. Aim 60–90 cm finished height with visible taper.

Budget wire: aluminium from a hardware store, roughly one-third the trunk thickness. Never leave wire to bite in over summer.

Project 1 · layout (use at build)

Forest rules for Aug 2027

Ignore this until Phase 4 — but tag trees in autumn so build day is fast.

  • Tallest tree slightly off-centre (not dead middle).
  • Cluster the next tallest nearby — a grove, not a fence line.
  • Shorter trees toward the front and edges; leave a clearing.
  • Vary lean; avoid parallel trunks.
  • Plant on a slight soil mound so flares can develop.
